# OLePFM Joint Action Plan — Annotated Skeleton Template

### Version 2 — September 2026

This document provides an annotated outline for compiling a Joint Action Plan (JAP) from multiple OLePFM sector diagnostic reports. Each section heading is followed by an annotation [in square brackets] explaining the purpose, content, length, and formatting conventions for that section. Annotations should be deleted from the final document. The structure follows the Malawi Joint Action Plan for Local Authority Service Delivery (post-workshop draft, September 2026) and the Uganda Joint Local Government Service Delivery Improvement Strategy 2027–2037.

## 3. Implementation Arrangements

[Purpose: Describe how the JAP will be implemented — the approach, the systems and capacity investment, the institutional architecture, and the monitoring framework. Approximately 3–4 pages total. Follow the subsection structure below precisely.]

### 3.1 Taking an Outcome-Led, Problem-Solving, Iterative Approach

[One paragraph of framing, then five bulleted features. Each feature is one to two sentences — specific enough to be meaningful, not so long as to become generic.]

The JAP will be implemented using the outcome-led, problem-solving approach set out in the OLePFM Synthesis Handbook. This approach has five defining features:

- Outcome-led and problem-focused. [Each reform action is tied to a specific bottleneck and measurable result. Progress is assessed against outcomes — not inputs or processes.]
- Joint ownership and action. [The JAP is a joint government document. Reforms requiring cross-ministerial action are driven through joint reform teams. Name the key joint ownership mechanisms in this country context.]
- User-centric design. [Solutions are developed with and tested by the officials and frontline workers who will use them. The sector diagnoses were prepared this way and implementation will continue it.]
- Iterative delivery. [Reforms are piloted, assessed, adjusted, and scaled progressively. Rolling five-action plans provide the operational basis for adaptive management.]
- Capability-matched solutions. [Reforms are designed to fit current institutional capacity with strengthening built in over time. Reforms requiring significant new capacity are sequenced after foundation-building steps.]

## Quick Reference: Formatting and Writing Conventions

[Document this section for reference during drafting. Delete entirely from the final document before sharing externally.]

Terminology:

- Objective — top-level grouping (do not use 'Pillar')
- Reform Result — specific change within each Objective (do not use 'Sub-Pillar')
- Action — specific steps in reform tables (do not use 'MPA')

Action numbering:

- Cross-sectoral: sequential within each Reform Result (1.1.1, 1.1.2, 1.1.3)
- Sector-specific: sector suffix (1.1.H for Health, 1.1.E for Education, 1.1.A for Agriculture)

Tables:

- Reform action tables: four columns (# | Action | Responsible | Timeframe)
- Results framework: five columns (Reform Result | Indicator | Baseline | Target | Data Source)
- Theory of change: Table 1 (objective × sector matrix); Table 2 (five-column results chain)

Citations: Bracketed inline (e.g. [MoH 2024], [PEFA 2026]). No footnotes.

Length guidance (excluding cover, ToC, abbreviations):

- Section 1 (Introduction and Reform Context): 5–7 pages
- Section 2 (Reform Strategy): 12–18 pages
- Section 3 (Implementation Arrangements): 3–4 pages
- Section 4 (Results Framework): 1–2 pages
- Total: approximately 25–35 pages for a three-sector JAP
