## PART G: CHAPTER 5 — CONCLUSION

### ✏️ Review the output — focus on these points

**Chapter 5 — Conclusion draft (G1).** Review before proceeding to compilation.

- [ ] Section 5.1 characterises the reform challenge, citing specific evidence
- [ ] Section 5.2 describes the logic and sequencing of the change objectives as an interconnected system
- [ ] Section 5.3 is honest about the Low-feasibility reforms and explains specifically what the reform plan does to address their political economy constraints
- [ ] Section 5.4 on development partners is analytically honest
- [ ] Section 5.5 correctly and completely identifies what the report does not cover
- [ ] Section 5.6 ends by connecting the reform objectives back to the human development outcome

Revise any section that is too generic, too optimistic, or inconsistent with the body of the report.

### Prompt G1 — Chapter 5: Conclusion

Using the analysis from Chapters 2, 3, and 4, and strictly following the Chapter 5 structure and introductory texts in the OLePFM Sector Reform Design Report Template, please draft Chapter 5 — Conclusion — for [country] in the [sector] sector.

Structure the chapter with the following sections:

**5.1 The Reform Challenge in Context (approximately 200 words):** Summarize the development gaps and their root cause in PFM failures. Name specific outcome indicators and link them back to the specific PFM bottlenecks identified in Chapter 3. Reference data from Section 2.1 and the OLePFM methodology from Chapter 1.

**5.2 The [Number] Reform Priorities and Their Logic (approximately 300 words):** Describe the change objectives as an interconnected system, not a list. Explain the sequencing logic: which reforms are foundational prerequisites; which address transmission mechanisms; which address specific operational input constraints; and which constitute the enabling environment. Use the bottleneck numbers and titles from Section 3.2.2.

**5.3 Sequencing, Feasibility, and Political Economy (approximately 200 words):** Summarize the feasibility ratings across the [total number] reform results (High/Medium/Low counts). Name the Low-feasibility reforms explicitly and explain specifically what the design of the reform plan does to address their political economy constraints.

**5.4 The Role of Development Partners (approximately 150 words):** Describe the specific ways in which development partner behaviour has contributed to fragmentation in [sector], and the phased approach for achieving consolidation. Be analytically honest.

**5.5 A Note on What This Report Does Not Cover (approximately 100 words):** Clearly demarcate any significant [sector] reform dimensions not covered by this analysis and that should be addressed in parallel.

**5.6 Conclusion (approximately 150 words):** End with a concise statement connecting the reform programme back to the human development outcome.

Cite all sources as hyperlinked parentheticals, e.g. ([World Bank, 2010](https://…)).

## QUALITY ASSURANCE PROMPTS

**QA1 — Template Compliance Check**

Please check that the section headings, subheadings, and introductory texts in the draft Chapters 4 and 5 strictly follow the OLePFM Sector Reform Design Report Template. Identify any deviations from the template structure and correct them.

**QA2 — Reform Result Consistency Check**

Please check that every reform result code, title, and feasibility rating is identical in: Section 4.1 narrative; the Section 4.1 summary table; Annex Table 3.1; and Chapter 5 Section 5.3. List any discrepancies.

**QA3 — Stakeholder Completeness Check**

Please check that every organization named as a key stakeholder in Section 4.2 also appears in Annex Tables 3.2 and 3.3. List any stakeholders missing from either table.

**QA4 — Sequencing Coherence Check**

Please review Annex Table 3.5 and identify: (i) any reform result whose Step 1 timeframe is later than a reform result that depends on it; (ii) any single organization with more than four lead assignments in the same year; and (iii) any Low-feasibility reform whose Step 3 falls within the first two years of the implementation horizon without adequate preparation steps.
