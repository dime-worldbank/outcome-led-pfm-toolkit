### Prompt 1-01 — Chapter 1: Introduction

Using the OLePFM Sector Reform Design Report Template, please provide a four-paragraph introduction to an Outcome-Led PFM Reform Diagnosis for [country] in the [sector] sector of no more than 350 words.

The introduction should:

(i) Briefly describe the specific outcome the analysis and action plan focuses on, and the government's policy objective and development challenge being addressed related to that outcome (no more than 80 words).

(ii) Explain the purpose of the outcome-led approach to PFM reform — using the standard text in the template exactly as written.

(iii) State the purpose of the report, adapting the standard text in the template to reflect: the country and sector context; the process on which the report is based (e.g. [process description]); and the intended use of the report (e.g. to inform a joint action plan, provide input to the PFM Reform Strategy, inform the design of a World Bank operation).

(iv) Briefly outline the structure of the report, following the standard roadmap text in the template.

### Prompt 1-02 — Section 2.1: Outcomes and Public Sector Results

Using the sources identified and the OLePFM Synthesis and relevant Sector Outcome Note as a guide, please provide an overview for [country] in the [sector] sector covering:

(i) Key development outcomes and indicators (e.g. mortality rates, learning poverty, agricultural productivity, coverage rates) — their current status, trends over time, and relationship to the government's policy objectives.

(ii) Public sector results in terms of standard access, quality, and efficiency measures (e.g. enrollment, completion, coverage rates, facility readiness, yield data) — what the public sector is delivering and where gaps exist.

Use the standard introductory text from the template to open the section. Keep the analysis to approximately 300 words, organized under the two sub-headings: Development Outcomes and Public Sector Results. Cite all sources as hyperlinked parentheticals, e.g. ([World Bank, 2010](https://…)).

## Sub-step 1.3 — Identify public sector challenges (Section 2.2)

### ✏️ Review the output — focus on these points

**Public Sector Challenges (Section 2.2, Prompt 1-03)** — the most important review in Chapter 2; these challenges drive the entire bottleneck analysis in Step 2.

- [ ] They're the most structurally important constraints on sector delivery in [country] — not just the most visible or politically salient
- [ ] Each challenge is distinct and covers a different dimension of the delivery problem
- [ ] They progress from the point of service delivery upward through the system
- [ ] No challenge mentions public finance or PFM
- [ ] The number of challenges (3–5) is appropriate

You can rename, merge, add, or reorder as needed. **Confirm the final list of challenge titles before proceeding to Section 2.3.**

### Prompt 1-03 — Section 2.2: Key Public Sector Challenges

Using the service delivery challenge categories in the [sector] OLePFM Outcome Note or the categorization in the OLePFM Synthesis as your organizing framework, please identify and describe preferably three and no more than five of the most important public sector service delivery challenges constraining [sector] development outcomes in [country].

For each challenge, provide:
(i) A title that describes the specific challenge faced in [country].
(ii) A paragraph of approximately 100 words describing the nature and evidence for the challenge.

Use the standard introductory text from the template to open the section. Ensure the challenges start from the point of delivery and move upward to central organizations. The challenges should be distinct from one another and together cover the main institutional, capacity, planning, coordination, and sustainability dimensions of the delivery problem. They should not simply restate resource constraints — they should explain why delivery falls short structurally. The challenges and their descriptions must not mention money, public finance, or PFM, but they can discuss the availability of and access to non-financial resources.
