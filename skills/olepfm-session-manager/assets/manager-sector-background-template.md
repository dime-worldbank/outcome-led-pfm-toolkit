## Chapter 1. Introduction

[One paragraph. The single development outcome this paper focuses on for [Country]; the current state of the sector in two or three sentences; and the government's policy intent as expressed in its main policy, vision and sector strategy documents, with citations.]

The outcome-led approach, rather than beginning with public financial management systems and asking how to improve them, begins with a development outcome and the public sector results needed to achieve it, and works backward to identify the specific PFM and institutional bottlenecks that stand in the way. It thereby concentrates reform effort on the changes in the management of public resources that matter most for delivery, rather than on PFM improvements pursued for their own sake.

[One paragraph. What the paper presents: an outcome-led diagnosis of the PFM and institutional bottlenecks constraining the outcome, and a prioritized, feasible reform action plan to resolve them. The evidence base it rests on (analytical, fiscal, political-economy and systems sources) and what it is intended to inform: a joint government and development-partner action plan, the PFM reform agenda, the design of operations.]

[Roadmap paragraph. Chapter 2 sets out the outcome, public sector results, service-delivery challenges and the policy, institutional, fiscal and feasibility context, which this background paper covers for the workshop.
