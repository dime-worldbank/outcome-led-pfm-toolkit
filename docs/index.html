Outcome-Led PFM Reform

OuR Assistant

Your AI navigator for outcome-led PFM reform—from design to delivery.

OuR Assistant is designed to help reform designers and facilitators diagnose problems and develop outcome-led PFM reforms. It guides them step by step through the reform design process, generates outcome reform design reports, and compiles joint action plans.

OuR Assistant skills

Four ways to use OuR Assistant

OuR Assistant has four skills which you can call on.

Stakeholder-Led Outcome Reform Design

Helps prepare workshop materials in advance of stakeholder workshops and uses the workshop outputs to draft the reform design report. OuR Assistant helps facilitators prepare background information and initially populate Working Tables for participants. It then uses completed working tables to draft the reform diagnosis and design chapters.

Output: a Stakeholder-Led Outcome Reform Design Report.

Research-Led Outcome Reform Design

Prepares a reform design report with AI assistance, using information available online and information provided by the user. OuR Assistant mirrors the workshop techniques used in reform diagnosis, but the reform designer is the sole human participant and must use their technical and contextual knowledge to guide the diagnosis and reform design. The more the researcher questions and interacts with OuR Assistant, the stronger the outcome.

Output: a Research-Led Outcome Reform Design Report.

Joint Bottleneck Identifier Available soon

Identifies bottlenecks common across outcomes. This skill supports the identification of joint bottlenecks at the end of Step 2, enabling a joint reform diagnosis and the design of reforms to resolve them.

Availability: coming soon. It will be an optional skill at the end of Step 2, when bottlenecks have been identified across multiple sectors.

Reform Action Plan Compilation and Generation

Prepares a reform plan based on one or more reform design reports. OuR Assistant uses the completed reports, helps the reform designer identify common cross-sectoral reforms, and drafts a combined reform plan for stakeholder validation and authorization.

Output: a Single or Joint Reform Action Plan.

How it works

The three steps in outcome-led reform design

The Outcome-Led approach sets out five steps in PFM reform design and implementation. OuR Assistant currently supports the first three steps of reform design.

1

Select outcomes, results and challenges

Establishes the scope and context for outcome-led PFM reform: priority development outcomes, related public sector results, and the main challenges to achieving them.

Output: Chapters 1 and 2 of the reform design report and the Step 1 annex tables.

Go to Step 1 →
2

Determine how public finance contributes and where it breaks down

Identifies the roles that public finance should play, diagnoses the PFM bottlenecks preventing those roles from being fulfilled, and prioritizes the most binding constraints.

Output: Chapter 3 of the reform design report.

Go to Step 2 →
3

Translate the diagnosis into an actionable reform plan

Reform teams examine the causes of bottlenecks, define what resolution would look like, identify reform results and milestones, and consolidate proposals into a coherent plan.

Outputs: the complete Outcome Reform Design Report, then a reform plan ready for stakeholder validation and authorization.

Go to Step 3 →

Interactive by design

The more you interact with OuR Assistant the more you will get out of it. It is not intended that users just accept OuR Assistant's output at each step as is, but to enquire, challenge and iterate and adapt the analysis and proposals from OuR Assistant. Question the challenges, sector analysis, bottlenecks, prioritization and structure until you are happy with the analysis and the supporting evidence. The analytical approach and report structure is a guide, but can be adapted, through interaction with OuR Assistant, and you can revert to earlier steps and revise and iterate at any time.

Step 1

Select Development Outcomes, Public Sector Results, and Challenges

Step 1 establishes the scope and context for outcome-led PFM reform. It identifies priority development outcomes, related public sector results, and the main challenges to achieving them. It also maps policies, institutions, financial flows, PFM systems, and key reform actors so that later analysis is grounded in context.

Outputs
  • Chapters 1 and 2 of the reform design report
  • The Step 1 annex tables, covering outcomes, public sector results, challenges, and sector context
Research-led design

This will take you, the designer, through Step 1 and generate Chapters 1 and 2 of the reform design report and the Step 1 annex table. It will help you establish the scope and contextual understanding before identifying the roles of public finance and priority bottlenecks in Step 2.

Stakeholder-led design

This will help you, the facilitator, prepare materials for the stakeholder reform design workshop. It will help participants agree on the scope, identify challenges, and provide contextual information to support validation of public finance roles and identification of priority bottlenecks in Step 2.

1.1

Select policy areas and the reform conveners and authorizers

In OuR Assistant. OuR Assistant starts by asking about the scope of the reform design areas and the nature of the reform process envisaged.

About this sub-step

The process starts by identifying who has the authority, convening power, and interest to lead or support reform; the reform entry points presented by existing processes and new opportunities; and the envisaged reform process and its outcome. This involves considering the roles of central agencies, sector actors, and other stakeholders, ensuring that the process begins with leadership, coordination, and sufficient political and bureaucratic support.

1.2

Select development outcomes and public sector results

In OuR Assistant. OuR Assistant will guide you through identifying the development outcomes and public sector results that form the focus of the reform design exercise. It will then look for relevant sources and ask you to upload any materials that may not be available online.

About this sub-step

This narrows the reform focus to a manageable set of priority outcomes and related public sector results that government can influence. It links broad policy ambitions to concrete areas of public action, ensuring that PFM reform is anchored in results rather than generic system improvement.

1.3

Identify existing or potential public sector challenges

In OuR Assistant. OuR Assistant will generate a proposed set of public sector challenges. Review them, suggest improvements, and prioritize them. Focusing on the problems that matter most will sharpen the reform design.

Output: Section 2.2
About this sub-step

This diagnoses the main obstacles preventing achievement of the selected results. It begins at the point of delivery, incorporating perspectives from users, frontline staff, managers, and central agencies. The purpose is to understand where public policies, capabilities, incentives, or institutions are failing in practice.

1.4

Map the sector policy, institutional, and public finance context

In OuR Assistant. OuR Assistant will help you map the public sector context step by step, starting with the overall public sector and institutional environment, then examining fiscal policy issues, financial flows, and relevant PFM systems and processes. Interaction is critical at each stage.

Output: Section 2.3
About this sub-step

This maps the policy environment, institutional responsibilities, delivery arrangements, fiscal policies, financial flows, and relevant PFM systems. It provides a structured picture of how government is organized to pursue the selected outcomes and how resources are mobilized, allocated, managed, and used in practice.

1.5

Assess institutional capability and policy feasibility

In OuR Assistant. Step 1 then generates a concluding Section 2.4, assessing institutional capability and policy feasibility to achieve the selected public sector results and outcomes.

Output: Section 2.4
About this sub-step

This assesses whether current policies are technically, politically, and financially feasible and whether institutions have the capability to deliver them. It tests the alignment among objectives, resources, incentives, delivery models, and operational capacity, helping distinguish realistic reform opportunities from ambitions that are unlikely to be implemented.

Research-led design

OuR Assistant will generate Chapters 1 and 2 of the reform design report and the Step 1 working tables for review by the researcher.

Stakeholder-led design

OuR Assistant will generate two outputs: first, an Outcome Context Brief with content similar to Chapters 1 and 2 of the reform design report; and second, initial content for the Step 1 Outcome Working Tables, for adjustment and validation by workshop participants. Following the workshop, the facilitator should upload the completed working tables and regenerate Step 1.

See the standard opening for an Outcome Context Brief

End of Step 1. OuR Assistant will now compile Chapters 1 and 2 and the Step 1 tables for the Annex, for your review and validation.

Step 2

Determine How Public Finance Contributes and Where It Breaks Down

Step 2 identifies the roles that public finance should play, diagnoses the PFM bottlenecks preventing those roles from being fulfilled, prioritizes the most binding constraints, and establishes reform teams to address them.

Output
  • Chapter 3 of the reform design report, setting out the roles of public finance and the PFM bottlenecks that will be the focus of reform design
Research-led design

This will take you, the designer, step by step through the analysis of public finance roles and the identification and prioritization of bottlenecks, generating Chapter 3 of the reform design report and the Step 2 annex tables.

Stakeholder-led design

OuR Assistant will generate an analysis of the roles of public finance for inclusion in the working tables and subsequent validation. Bottlenecks will then be identified and prioritized during the workshop and recorded in the Step 2 working tables. Once the workshop is complete, the facilitator uploads the working tables and OuR Assistant drafts Chapter 3 of the report.

2.1

Assess the potential and actual roles of public finance

In OuR Assistant. OuR Assistant will draft this analysis for Section 3.1 using the contextual analysis and available sources and will complete Table 2.1.

Output: Section 3.1Table 2.1
About this sub-step

This substep examines how fiscal policy and PFM systems support feasible policy and effective delivery. It considers how public finance can build commitment, sustain fiscal space, mobilize and distribute resources strategically, and strengthen productivity, transparency, and accountability in delivery within the specific sector and country context.

Research-led design

Review the tables and suggest improvements to OuR Assistant.

Stakeholder-led design

Participants will be asked to validate the identified roles during the workshop. If they make changes, regenerate the narrative for Section 3.1.

2.2

Identify and prioritize PFM bottlenecks

In OuR Assistant. The process first identifies the bottlenecks that contribute to service delivery challenges in Table 2.2. It then applies criteria relating to the impact of those bottlenecks and the feasibility of resolving them to prioritize the PFM bottlenecks in Table 2.3. The output is Section 3.2, which sets out the priority bottlenecks that will be the focus of reform diagnosis and design.

Output: Section 3.2Tables 2.2 and 2.3
About this sub-step

This substep traces public sector challenges back to specific PFM bottlenecks. It prioritizes bottlenecks based on their impact on results, cross-sector relevance, and feasibility of resolution, producing a limited and manageable reform focus.

Research-led design

OuR Assistant will take you through the bottleneck identification and prioritization process, with opportunities for user interaction, and will then draft Section 3.2 of the report.

Stakeholder-led design

During the workshop, participants will identify and prioritize bottlenecks through participatory exercises described in the working tables. Upload the completed Tables 2.2 and 2.3, and OuR Assistant will draft Section 3.2 of the report.

Optional, at this point. If you have identified bottlenecks across multiple sectors, you may wish to identify joint bottlenecks that can be addressed across sectors and form the focus of Step 3. This is an optional additional skill, the Joint Bottleneck Identifier, which will be available soon.

End of Step 2. OuR Assistant will now compile Chapter 3 and the Step 2 tables for the Annex, for your review and validation.

Step 3

Translate the Diagnosis into an Actionable Reform Plan

Reform teams examine the causes of bottlenecks, define what resolution would look like, identify reform results and milestones, align responsibilities and stakeholder strategies, consolidate proposals into a coherent plan, and secure authorization for implementation.

Outputs
  • First, a complete Outcome Reform Design Report. OuR Assistant completes Chapter 4 and the Step 3 annex tables, covering the reform diagnosis and proposed reforms, stakeholder roles and management strategy, and the approach to systems, capacity, and technical assistance. OuR Assistant then compiles the report.
  • Second, a reform plan, which can be prepared from one or more reform design reports and is ready for stakeholder validation and authorization.
3.1

Explore causes and identify reforms to resolve PFM bottlenecks

In OuR Assistant. Whether the design is research-led or stakeholder-led, this sub-step involves: (i) using the Five Whys technique to identify the causes of bottlenecks; (ii) applying the lenses of feasibility, functionality, and relevance to identify reforms; and (iii) carrying out stakeholder analysis.

Output: Sections 4.1 and 4.2Working Tables 3.1, 3.2 and 3.3
About this sub-step

This substep helps reform teams develop a shared understanding of why each bottleneck exists. Teams define the bottleneck and examine its root causes, including the underlying institutional and policy interactions. They then define what a resolved bottleneck would look like and identify the reforms needed in behavior, systems, processes, and organizations, together with the required reform results. They assess stakeholder incentives, authority, and capabilities, creating a stronger basis for practical and politically feasible reforms and for identifying stakeholder roles and management strategies.

Research-led design

OuR Assistant takes the user through virtual exercises in reform diagnosis and design.

Stakeholder-led design

Stakeholders carry out the exercises in a workshop setting and then upload the completed working tables. OuR Assistant will then generate Sections 4.1 and 4.2 of the report.

3.2

Identify digital systems, capacity, and technical assistance needs

In OuR Assistant. OuR Assistant generates the table and drafts the analysis based on the prior reform diagnosis and design and available sources, for validation by the researcher or stakeholders.

Output: Section 4.3Working Table 3.4
About this sub-step

Teams then develop stakeholder strategies and identify the digital systems, capacity, and technical assistance needed to achieve functional change.

Completion of the Outcome Reform Design Report. OuR Assistant will now compile Chapter 4 and the Step 3 tables for the Annex, for your review. It will then compile the full reform design report and further request you to review and validate the full report with outcome stakeholders.

3.3

Compile, refine, and ensure coherence and feasibility

In OuR Assistant. This sub-step is common to research-led and stakeholder-led design. It involves:

  1. uploading working tables and other source documents and searching for supplementary context;
  2. synthesizing common bottlenecks;
  3. developing the objective and reform-result structure;
  4. drafting the joint action plan section by section.
Output: the reform action plan
About this sub-step

This substep consolidates team plans into one coherent reform plan and theory of change, either a single-sector plan or a joint reform plan. Conveners and reform teams check consistency, complementarity, feasibility, capacity needs, system implications, and stakeholder strategies across bottlenecks, ensuring that the overall reform package is manageable, aligned, and realistic before seeking high-level approval.

Completion of Step 3. OuR Assistant will generate the full reform action plan for stakeholder validation, review and iteration.

Part of the toolkit for Outcome-Led Reform

OuR Assistant forms part of the toolkit for Outcome-Led Reform. In particular, please refer to the OLePFM Practitioners Toolkit Guidance Note, the Reform Design and Planning Documents, and Collaborative Reform Tools. You can also visit worldbank.org/en/programs/reimagining-public-finance for news about the OLePFM approach.

Standard opening for an Outcome Context Brief (stakeholder-led, Step 1)

The brief opens with the text below, with the bracketed items filled in for the engagement.

The purpose of this brief is to provide preliminary background information to facilitate stakeholder diagnosis of the key institutional and PFM bottlenecks preventing public finance from playing its necessary role in [enter policy area here].

The report sets out the outcome context and key public sector delivery challenges and describes the institutional, financial, and PFM environment. It is based on a desk review of recent [sector/outcome] analyses and data and PFM diagnostics, including [mention sources here], supplemented by AI-assisted analysis.

The Outcome-Led approach to PFM reform (OLePFM) used in this process (World Bank 2026) starts with the development outcomes that public finance must help deliver. It works backward from desired results to identify the role of public finance and the specific institutional and PFM bottlenecks that constrain achievement of those results. For example, bottlenecks may prevent resources from reaching the point of service delivery in adequate amounts, at the right time, and with sufficient accountability.

The reform design process is intended to inform [provide details of the specific country reform process].
