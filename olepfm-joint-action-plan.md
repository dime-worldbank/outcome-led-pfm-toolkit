This skill guides the compilation of a Joint Action Plan (JAP) from multiple OLePFM sector diagnostic reports. The output document follows the OLePFM Joint Action Plan Annotated Skeleton Template, which should be uploaded alongside the sector diagnostic reports at the start of each task. The structural framework — its objectives and reform results — is drawn from one or both of two PFM reform frameworks, selected based on the sectors and outcomes in scope.

---

## On Activation

When this skill is triggered, begin by confirming with the user:
1. Which sector diagnostic reports are available (ask the user to upload them if not already in the conversation).
2. Whether the OLePFM Joint Action Plan Annotated Skeleton Template is available. If not, ask the user to upload it — it contains the document structure, formatting conventions, and writing guidance that governs the output.
3. The country or region context and the name of the national PFM strategy the JAP should feed into.
4. Whether supplementary sources should be searched (internal WBG intranet and web) or whether the skill should work from uploaded documents only.

Do not begin drafting until all source documents are confirmed and uploaded.

---

## Step 1: Read and Extract from Each Sector Diagnostic

For each uploaded OLePFM sector diagnostic report, use document_chat to extract:
- Development outcomes and public sector results targeted
- Key public sector challenges
- Priority PFM bottlenecks: for each, extract the bottleneck name, underlying causes, impact and feasibility ratings, relevant stakeholders, and the change objective it maps to
- Change objectives and reform results: for each change objective, list all reform results and proposed reforms
- The action plan matrix from the annex (columns: sub-bottleneck / underlying causes / stakeholders / reform result / reforms required)
- The systems, capacity and TA section: extract all digital systems priorities, capacity development priorities, and TA packages identified and their links to specific reform results

Produce a structured internal summary of each sector's content before proceeding. Do not present this to the user unless requested.

---

## Step 2: Search for Supplementary Context (if authorised)

Run parallel searches on the WBG intranet and web for:
- Recent PFM assessments (PEFA, public expenditure reviews)
- Intergovernmental fiscal transfer and local government financing data
- Local authority performance assessments
- National development strategy and PFM strategy documents
- Relevant World Bank project documentation

Summarise key findings for the user, identifying which sources will be drawn on and for which sections.

---

## Step 3: Synthesise Common Bottlenecks

Read across all sector extractions to identify bottlenecks appearing in two or more sector reports. For each cross-cutting cluster:
- Name the cluster in clear, non-technical language stakeholders will recognise
- List which sectors it appears in and the sector-specific manifestation with precise figures
- Note sector-specific bottlenecks that do not appear cross-sectorally

Present the bottleneck synthesis to the user in a table before proceeding, and invite confirmation.

---

## Step 4: Select the Reform Framework and Design the Objective and Reform Result Structure

The structural architecture of the JAP — its objectives and reform results — is guided by one or both of two PFM reform frameworks, selected based on the sectors and development outcomes in scope. Both use the same terminology: **Objective** (top-level grouping) → **Reform Result** (specific change within each objective).

**Framework 1 — PFM for Service Delivery.** Use when the JAP addresses sectors focused on frontline service delivery outcomes (health, education, water, agriculture, social protection). Three objectives and nine reform results:
- Objective 1 — Financing Service Delivery: (1.1) Needs-based resource allocation; (1.2) Reliable financing flows; (1.3) Financial management at facility and local level.
- Objective 2 — Putting Resources to Work: (2.1) Human resources; (2.2) Operational inputs; (2.3) Infrastructure and equipment.
- Objective 3 — Participation, Management and Accountability for Results: (3.1) Performance management; (3.2) Citizen engagement; (3.3) Integrated information.

**Framework 2 — PFM for Jobs and Resilience.** Use when the JAP addresses sectors focused on fiscal sustainability, economic growth, revenue mobilisation, or public investment outcomes. Four objectives and thirteen reform results:
- Objective 1 — Domestic Revenue Mobilisation: (1.1) Revenue policy and medium-term revenue strategy; (1.2) Tax administration modernisation; (1.3) Compliance risk management and taxpayer engagement.
- Objective 2 — Fiscal Sustainability and Resilience: (2.1) Medium-term fiscal framework and budgeting; (2.2) Expenditure rationalisation; (2.3) Treasury and cash management; (2.4) Fiscal risk management.
- Objective 3 — Public Investment, Infrastructure and Procurement: (3.1) Public investment management, PPPs and project management; (3.2) Asset management and maintenance; (3.3) Procurement and private sector development; (3.4) SOE governance and utility management.
- Objective 4 — Transparency, Accountability and Enabling Environment: (4.1) Fiscal transparency; (4.2) Audit and oversight; (4.3) Accounting and data systems.

**Selecting the framework:**
- All sectors are service delivery → use Framework 1 only
- All sectors are economic/fiscal → use Framework 2 only
- JAP spans both → use a combination, consolidating overlapping elements

Present the proposed objective and reform result structure to the user for approval before drafting. If it is not immediately clear which framework applies, present both with a brief explanation and ask the user to confirm.

## Step 5: Draft the Joint Action Plan Document

Use the OLePFM Joint Action Plan Annotated Skeleton Template as the master guide for structure, format, and writing conventions. The template contains section-by-section annotations explaining purpose, content, length, and formatting for each part of the document. Follow it precisely.

The document sections are:

- Cover page
- Table of Contents
- Abbreviations (two-column table)
- **A. Introduction and Reform Context** — subsections: I. Purpose; II. Country ambition; III. The problem (including Box 1 with sector-by-sector outcome statistics)
- **B. Problem Statement** — subsections: I. A common pattern; II. The binding constraints (two groups matching the objective logic, each group containing numbered bottleneck descriptions)
- **C. Theory of Change** — two to three paragraphs plus Table 1 (sector reform results by objective) and Table 2 (five-column results chain: Bottleneck → Reform Actions → Reform Results → Public Sector Results → Development Outcomes)
- **D. Reform Objectives and Actions** — organised by Objective → Reform Result, each Reform Result using the standard format: The problem / The reform result (bolded) / Cross-sectoral reforms table (3 columns: # | Action | Responsible) / Sector-specific reforms table (same format, actions labelled with sector suffix e.g. 1.1.H, 1.1.E, 1.1.A)
- **E. Implementation Arrangements** — five subsections: I. Outcome-led approach (five features); II. Systems, capacity and TA (three sub-headings: digital systems / capacity development / TA); III. Multi-stakeholder institutional arrangements (four tiers: steering / technical committee / reform teams / coalitions); IV. Monitoring and evaluation (two layers: progress monitoring / impact assessment); V. Relationship with ongoing reform processes
- **F. Reform Results Framework** — five-column table (Reform Result | Indicator | Baseline | Target | Data Source), organised by Objective and Reform Result, cross-sectoral indicators only, all baselines TBD
- Bibliography

Do not include annexes.

**Key formatting and writing conventions (from the skeleton template):**
- Use **Objective** for top-level groupings; **Reform Result** for changes within each objective; **Action** for specific steps in tables. Do not use 'Pillar', 'Sub-Pillar', or 'MPA'.
- Cross-sectoral action numbering: sequential within each reform result (e.g. 1.1.1, 1.1.2)
- Sector-specific action numbering: sector suffix (e.g. 1.1.H, 1.1.E, 1.1.A)
- All figures cited with bracketed source (e.g. [MoH 2024], [PEFA 2026])
- No footnotes — all citations inline
- Target length: 25–35 pages for a three-sector JAP

**Content guidance for Section E.II — Systems, Capacity and TA:**
Draft from the systems, capacity, and TA content extracted in Step 1. Digital systems: identify highest-priority systems reforms that are technical preconditions for critical reform results; explain why interoperability matters; note sequencing. Capacity development: identify binding gaps at central ministry, oversight body, and local authority/frontline levels. TA: sequence ahead of the instruments it enables; deliver through government systems not parallel units; link each package explicitly to reform results. If a diagnostic has no explicit systems/capacity/TA section, infer requirements from the reform actions.

**Content guidance for Section E.V — Relationship with Ongoing Reform Processes:**
Name all specific reform processes the JAP coordinates with — national PFM strategy, sector programmes, development partner projects, intergovernmental reform processes. Note the specific interface with each. Generic language is not sufficient.

---

## Step 6: Cross-Check Completeness

Compare the JAP against each sector diagnostic to identify:
(a) **Gaps** — bottlenecks or problems not addressed (list with source and section)
(b) **Missing actions** — proposed reforms not captured (table: action | source | diagnostic change objective | recommended JAP reform result)
(c) **Additions** — content not grounded in diagnostics; flag as appropriate system-level enablers or beyond the evidence base

Present as a structured gap analysis with recommendations.

---

## Step 7: Revise

Apply targeted edits using edit_document. Use create_document only if structural reorganisation affects more than half the document. Track which version is current.

---

## Step 8: Finalise

Check:
- All baselines TBD with note on establishment process
- All placeholders clearly marked
- Abbreviations table complete
- Action numbering consistent (cross-sectoral sequential; sector-specific with suffix)
- Lead agencies named consistently
- Title, version, date correct
- Terminology consistent: Objective / Reform Result / Action; no Pillar / Sub-Pillar / MPA
- Systems, capacity and TA in E.II explicitly linked to reform results in D
- No annexes included
- Bibliography complete with all cited sources

---

## Edge Cases

- **Fewer than three sector diagnoses:** Proceed but flag synthesis is less robust; JAP will need updating as additional diagnoses are completed.
- **Sector diagnoses using different OLePFM templates:** Adapt extraction; map all content to the same synthesis framework.
- **Skeleton template not uploaded:** Ask the user to upload it before proceeding. If unavailable, use the Malawi post-workshop JAP (September 2026) as the format reference.
- **User wants to include sectors without completed diagnoses:** Note as 'diagnosis in preparation' in Section A and Box 1.
- **Five or more sector diagnoses:** Limit to three or four objectives and eight to twelve reform results; group closely related bottlenecks.
- **Ambiguous framework selection:** Present both frameworks with brief explanation; ask user to confirm.
