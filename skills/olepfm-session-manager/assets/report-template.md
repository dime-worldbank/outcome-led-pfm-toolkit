The Outcome-Led approach to PFM reform (OLePFM) used in this diagnosis (World Bank 2026) starts with the development outcomes that public finance must help deliver.
It works backward from desired results to identify the role of public finance in and the specific institutional and PFM bottlenecks that constrain the achieving of those results – for example, preventing resources from reaching the point of service delivery in adequate quantity, at the right time, and with sufficient accountability.

# 2. Outcome and Public Sector Context (Step 1)

The first step in reform design is to set out the agreed scope and context for an outcome-led PFM reform agenda.
This involves the identification of a set of desired development outcomes that the government intends to pursue, and then the public sector results that are the focus of government efforts to achieve them – which will become the focus of PFM reform.
This is followed by a mapping of the relevant government policies and institutions, i.e. how the public sector is organized to deliver them, and finally the fiscal policy and PFM context.
The section concludes with an assessment of the institutional capability and feasibility of delivering policy objectives.
